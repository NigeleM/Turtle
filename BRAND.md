# Turtle name and logo policy

Turtle's code is licensed under the [Apache License 2.0](LICENSE), which
lets anyone use, change and share it. The **name and logo** are different:
the name "Turtle" for this programming language, and the Turtle logo,
identify the official project, created by **Nigele McCoy**. The Apache
License does not grant any right to use them (section 6). This policy
says how you may use them, so that people can always tell the official
Turtle from other things.

## You may, without asking

- **Say what your work is written in or works with**: "written in
  Turtle", "a Turtle library", "for Turtle", "Turtle support for
  VS Code".
- **Share official Turtle unchanged** (the releases, or a copy of this
  repository) under its name, with its LICENSE and NOTICE.
- **Write about Turtle**: articles, books, courses, talks and reviews may
  use the name, as long as they don't suggest they're official or
  endorsed.
- **Name a program you wrote in Turtle anything you like**, as long as
  the name isn't "Turtle" itself or likely to be confused with it.

## You need permission for

- **A changed version of Turtle under the Turtle name.** A fork or
  modified build must use a different name (you may say it's "based on
  Turtle").
- **Product, company, project or domain names** that use "Turtle" in a
  way people could take for the official project ("Turtle Studio",
  "TurtleLang Pro").
- **The Turtle logo**, or anything that looks like it, beyond showing it
  unchanged next to a mention of Turtle.
- **Saying or implying** that Nigele McCoy or the Turtle project endorses
  or works with your product.

## How to ask

Open an issue at https://github.com/NigeleM/Turtle with what you'd like to
do. Permission is usually given for uses that help people find and use
Turtle and don't confuse them about what's official.

This policy may change as Turtle grows. It isn't legal advice; when in
doubt, ask.
